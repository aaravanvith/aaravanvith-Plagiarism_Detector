Empowered Relief for Youth: Development, feasibility and early effectiveness of a single session pain relief skills class.

Empowered Relief (ER) is a single-session pain management class that teaches pain science education and self-regulatory skills and has demonstrated efficacy in adults with chronic pain. We adapted ER for youth with chronic pain (Empowered Relief for Youth [ERY]) using patient partner and clinician input and evaluated feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary effectiveness in a single-arm clinical trial (NCT05998369). Among 236 youth referred from a tertiary pain clinic, 110 youth ages 10-18 years (M=14.8, SD=2.1) enrolled and completed ERY. Primary outcomes were feasibility and acceptability; secondary outcomes included pain interference, pain catastrophizing, anxiety, pain self-efficacy, and patient global impression of change assessed at 1-, 2-, and 3-month follow-up. Over 80% of participants rated ERY as credible, acceptable, and feasible and anticipated clinically meaningful improvement. Participants reported preliminary improvements in pain interference, pain catastrophizing, and anxiety from baseline to 1 month that were observed through 3 months. Pain self-efficacy improved at 2-month follow-up. Exploratory analyses showed a significant time-by-timing interaction for pain interference, with greater early improvement among youth who attended ERY within 30 days of their pain clinic evaluation compared to later attendance. These findings support ERY as a feasible, acceptable, and potentially promising brief behavioral intervention for youth with chronic pain.
