Emergence of gene associations in high-risk K. pneumoniae clones: blaNDM-1 + blaOXA-48-like in ST15 and blaVIM + blaNDM-1 in ST405.

Immunocompromised patients, especially hematopoietic stem cell transplants (HSCT) / bone marrow transplant recipients, are at risk for colonization and infection with carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CR-KP) which is associated with increased bloodstream infections and mortality in this population. In this susceptible population, we investigated the molecular epidemiology of CR-KP. The strains isolated over a five-year period (2017-2021) were then characterized by PCR and sequencing targeting carbapenemase genes. ERIC-PCR was utilized to examine clonal relations, while multilocus sequence typing was carried out on representative isolates. Analysis of 142 CR-KP uncovered remarkable genetic heterogeneity with six predominant clusters making up for 83,8% of strains and one major cluster. Novel findings in K. pneumoniae included: undescribed MLST profile ST5187, emergence of blaVIM-1 in ST397, emergence of blaOXA-48-like in ST219, co-carriage of either blaNDM-1 + blaOXA-48-like in ST15 or blaVIM-like + blaNDM-1 in ST405. CR-KP, brought to light new carbapenemase gene associations and undescribed MLST profiles. To prevent transmission of high-risk clones in immunocompromised populations, enhanced molecular surveillance and robust infection control are of high importance.
